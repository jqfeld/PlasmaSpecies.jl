Reactions

Two types. A ReactionFormula is stoichiometry only — which species, which coefficients, reversible or not. A PlasmaReaction is a formula with a rate attached.

Parsing

julia> using PlasmaSpecies
julia> r = p"e + N2 --> 2e + N2[+]"e+N2-->2e+N2[+]
julia> r.subs, r.substoich(Species{S, C, Nothing, Nothing, Nothing, Nothing, Nothing, Nothing} where {S, C<:Charge}[e, N2], [1, 1])
julia> r.prods, r.prodstoich(Species{S, C, Nothing, Nothing, Nothing, Nothing, Nothing, Nothing} where {S, C<:Charge}[e, N2[+]], [2, 1])
julia> r.reversefalse

The p"..." macro returns a ReactionFormula when the string contains an arrow and a Species otherwise; parse_reaction is the function form. Terms are separated by +, and a + inside brackets (N2[+]) is not a separator.

Three arrows:

julia> p"N2[X,vib=0] <--> N2[X,vib=1]"     # reversible, sets r.reverseN2[X,vib=0]<-->N2[X,vib=1]
julia> parse_reaction("N2[X,vib=1] <-- N2[X,vib=0]")   # normalised to forwardN2[X,vib=0]-->N2[X,vib=1]

Coefficients may be fractional, which is common for wall and surface reactions. A decimal anywhere on a side promotes that side's coefficients to Float64; otherwise they stay Int:

julia> f = parse_reaction("O[X] + 0.5O2[X] --> O3")0.5O2[X]+O[X]-->O3
julia> f.substoich2-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.5
 1.0

Both sides are normalised on construction: repeated species are combined and species are sorted, so formulas that mean the same thing print and compare the same.

julia> parse_reaction("e + e + N2 --> N2")2e+N2-->N2

Expanding over a tree

A chemistry is usually written over lumped species and solved over resolved ones. apply_tree does the expansion: every species in the formula is resolved to the leaves it matches, and one reaction is emitted per combination.

julia> t = SpeciesTree(["e", "N2[X,vib=0]", "N2[X,vib=1]", "N2[X,vib=2]", "N2[+]"])nothing
├─ e
├─ N2
│  └─ N2[X]
│     ├─ N2[X,vib=0]
│     ├─ N2[X,vib=1]
│     └─ N2[X,vib=2]
└─ N2[+]
julia> apply_tree(t, p"e + N2[X,vib=0-1] --> e + N2[X,vib=2]")2-element Vector{Tuple{Float64, ReactionFormula}}:
 (1.0, e+N2[X,vib=0]-->e+N2[X,vib=2])
 (1.0, e+N2[X,vib=1]-->e+N2[X,vib=2])

Each result carries a branching factor of 1 / (number of product combinations): the rate is split equally over the possible outcomes. Writing a product as a lumped species therefore distributes it:

julia> apply_tree(t, p"e + N2[X,vib=0] --> e + N2[X]")3-element Vector{Tuple{Float64, ReactionFormula}}:
 (0.3333333333333333, e+N2[X,vib=0]-->e+N2[X,vib=0])
 (0.3333333333333333, e+N2[X,vib=0]-->e+N2[X,vib=1])
 (0.3333333333333333, e+N2[X,vib=0]-->e+N2[X,vib=2])

For a PlasmaReaction the factor is folded into the rate by scale_rate instead, and the result is a vector of PlasmaReaction:

julia> pr = PlasmaReaction(1.5e-16, p"e + N2[X,vib=0-1] --> e + N2[X,vib=2]")PlasmaReaction{Float64, ReactionFormula}(1.5e-16, e+N2[X,vib=0-1]-->e+N2[X,vib=2])
julia> [(x.rate, x.formula) for x in apply_tree(t, pr)]2-element Vector{Tuple{Float64, ReactionFormula}}:
 (1.5e-16, e+N2[X,vib=0]-->e+N2[X,vib=2])
 (1.5e-16, e+N2[X,vib=1]-->e+N2[X,vib=2])

A species that matches nothing in the tree would silently expand the reaction away, so instead the reaction is dropped with a warning. Passing several reactions concatenates the results and skips any that throw.

Expansion can produce identities (A --> A) when a lumped reaction collapses onto one state. isreactive filters them out.

Rates

PlasmaReaction.rate is deliberately untyped. A number is a constant rate coefficient; anything callable is left to the consumer to evaluate, so a fit in gas temperature or reduced field, or an interpolation from a Boltzmann solver, works unchanged. scale_rate is the only place this package touches a rate, and it handles both:

julia> scale_rate(2.0, 0.5)1.0
julia> scale_rate(Te -> 1e-15 * Te^0.5, 0.5)(4.0)1.0e-15

Energetics

Give the species energies — usually with apply_energy! over a tree — and a reaction's energy balance follows:

julia> tv = SpeciesTree(["N2[X,vib=0]", "N2[X,vib=1]"]);
julia> apply_energy!(tv, q -> 0.29 * q.vib);
julia> lv = leaves(tv)2-element Vector{Species{Molecule{Tuple{Pair{Element, Int64}}, Tuple{Pair{Element, Int64}}}, Neutral, StringElectronicState, Int64, Nothing, Float64, Nothing, Nothing}}:
 N2[X,vib=0]
 N2[X,vib=1]
julia> pr = PlasmaReaction(2.0, ReactionFormula([lv[1]], [lv[2]], [1], [1], false))PlasmaReaction{Float64, ReactionFormula}(2.0, N2[X,vib=0]-->N2[X,vib=1])
julia> reaction_energy(pr)0.29
julia> thermal_source_term(pr)(1e20)-5.8e19

reaction_energy is Σ E(products) - Σ E(substrates), negative for an exothermic reaction, and nothing if any energy is unset. thermal_source_term builds the gas-heating term rate * ∏ nᵢ^stoichᵢ * (-ΔE) as a function of the substrate densities, in the order of pr.formula.subs.

Two consistency checks are available, though not exported: PlasmaSpecies.ismassbalanced and PlasmaSpecies.ischargebalanced.

julia> PlasmaSpecies.ischargebalanced(p"e + N2 --> 2e + N2[+]")true

Export to Catalyst

Loading Catalyst.jl activates an extension providing to_catalyst. Species variables are named after the species string, so anything with a charge or state label becomes a var"..." identifier.

julia> using PlasmaSpecies, Catalyst

julia> t = SpeciesTree(["e", "N2[X,vib=0]", "N2[X,vib=1]", "N2[+]"]);

julia> rs = to_catalyst(t, (1.0, p"e + N2[X] --> 2e + N2[+]"))
2-element Vector{Reaction{...}}:
 1.0, e + var"N2[X,vib=0]" --> 2*e + var"N2[+]"
 1.0, e + var"N2[X,vib=1]" --> 2*e + var"N2[+]"

julia> ReactionSystem(rs, default_t(); name = :plasma)
Model plasma:
Unknowns (4): see unknowns(plasma)
  e(t)
  var"N2[X,vib=0]"(t)
  var"N2[+]"(t)
  var"N2[X,vib=1]"(t)

Reactions are given as (rate, formula) tuples or PlasmaReactions, singly, as varargs or as a vector, and are expanded over the tree on the way out. A SpeciesTree on its own converts to the sum of its leaf variables.

PlasmaSpecies.ReactionFormula — Type
ReactionFormula(subs, prods, substoich, prodstoich, reverse)
ReactionFormula(str::AbstractString)

Stoichiometry of one reaction: substrate and product species with their coefficients, plus whether it is reversible. No rate — pair it with one using PlasmaReaction.

Fields

  • subs, prods: Vector{Species}.
  • substoich, prodstoich: coefficients, Int throughout unless a fractional one appears, which promotes the whole side to Float64.
  • reverse::Bool: true for <-->.

The constructor normalises both sides: repeated species are combined (e + e becomes 2e) and species are sorted, so two formulas written differently but meaning the same thing compare and print identically.

From a string, see parse_reaction; the @p_str macro is the short form.

A ReactionFormula with a rate attached.

rate is deliberately untyped: a number is a constant rate coefficient, anything callable is evaluated by the consumer (a temperature- or field-dependent fit, an interpolation from a Boltzmann solver). Only scale_rate touches it here.

PlasmaSpecies.parse_reaction — Function
parse_reaction(str) -> ReactionFormula

Parse a reaction string in LoKI-B notation. Sides are separated by an arrow and terms by +:

e + N2 --> 2e + N2[+]
N2[X,vib=0] <--> N2[X,vib=1]     # reversible
N2[X,vib=1] <-- N2[X,vib=0]      # normalised to the forward direction

Each term is an optional stoichiometric coefficient followed by a species string (see Species(::String)). A decimal coefficient (0.5O2[X], common in wall reactions) makes that side Float64. Whitespace is ignored, and a + inside brackets (N2[+]) is not a term separator.

source

PlasmaSpecies.apply_tree — Function
apply_tree(t::SpeciesTree, r::ReactionFormula)  -> Vector{Tuple{Float64,ReactionFormula}}
apply_tree(t::SpeciesTree, pr::PlasmaReaction)  -> Vector{PlasmaReaction}

Expand a reaction written over lumped species into one reaction per combination of actual states in t.

Every species in the formula is resolved to the leaves of t it matches, via matching_leaves — an exact state matches itself, a lumped one (N2[X]) matches its whole subtree, and a range (vib=0-4) matches the levels it contains. The result is one reaction per element of the product of those sets.

Each gets a branching factor of 1 / (number of product combinations): the total rate is split equally over the possible outcomes. For a ReactionFormula the factor is returned alongside the reaction; for a PlasmaReaction it is folded into the rate by scale_rate.

Reactions whose species are not in the tree at all are dropped with a warning, rather than silently expanding to nothing. Passing several reactions (varargs or a vector) concatenates the results and skips any that throw, again with a warning.

julia> t = SpeciesTree(["e", "N2[X,vib=0]", "N2[X,vib=1]", "N2[X,vib=2]"]);

julia> apply_tree(t, p"e + N2[X,vib=0-1] --> e + N2[X,vib=2]")
2-element Vector{Tuple{Float64, ReactionFormula}}:
 (1.0, e+N2[X,vib=0]-->e+N2[X,vib=2])
 (1.0, e+N2[X,vib=1]-->e+N2[X,vib=2])

PlasmaSpecies.scale_rate — Function
scale_rate(rate, factor)

Scale a reaction rate by a branching factor. A number scales directly; anything else is treated as callable and wrapped, so rate coefficients that depend on temperature or reduced field survive apply_tree.

source
PlasmaSpecies.isreactive — Function
isreactive(r) -> Bool

Whether a ReactionFormula or PlasmaReaction changes anything: false when both sides hold the same species with the same coefficients. apply_tree can produce such identities when a lumped reaction expands onto a single state, and they contribute nothing to a chemistry.

source
PlasmaSpecies.reaction_energy — Function
reaction_energy(r::PlasmaReaction) -> Union{Number,Nothing}

Energy balance Σ E(products) - Σ E(substrates), weighted by stoichiometry. Negative for an exothermic reaction. Returns nothing if any species has no energy set, which is the expected state for a chemistry that has not had apply_energy! applied.

The unit is whatever energy carries.

source
PlasmaSpecies.thermal_source_term — Function
thermal_source_term(pr::PlasmaReaction) -> Function

Build (n₁, n₂, ...) -> rate * ∏ nᵢ^stoichᵢ * (-ΔE), the power released into the gas by pr at the given substrate densities. Arguments are the substrate densities in the order of pr.formula.subs.

Sign convention: an exothermic reaction (ΔE < 0, see reaction_energy) gives a positive source. Errors unless every species in the reaction has an energy; set them with apply_energy!.

Assumes a constant rate — the product is formed directly, without evaluating a callable rate.

PlasmaSpecies.to_catalyst — Function
to_catalyst(sp::Species)
to_catalyst(t::SpeciesTree)
to_catalyst(t::SpeciesTree, reactions...)

Export to Catalyst.jl. Provided by an extension: it only works once Catalyst is loaded in the same session, otherwise every method throws.

  • A Species becomes a Catalyst species variable named after its string, so anything carrying a charge or a state label is a var"..." identifier (var"N2[+]", var"N2[X,vib=1]").
  • A SpeciesTree becomes the sum of its leaf variables.
  • reactions are (rate, formula) tuples or PlasmaReactions, given as varargs or a vector. Each is expanded over the tree by apply_tree — branching factors folded into the rate — and returned as a Vector{Reaction} ready for a ReactionSystem. Reactions that throw are skipped with a warning.
